## What the model is

* **Task**: binary classification. Input: one loan application. Output: a score between 0 and 1
  for the applicant turning out *bad* (not repaying), and a HIGH / LOW risk decision at a threshold.
* **Model**: `random_forest` inside a scikit-learn `Pipeline`
  (`StandardScaler` for 6 numeric columns, `OneHotEncoder` for
  11 categorical columns). Both candidates used `class_weight="balanced"`.
* **Selection**: 5-fold stratified cross-validation on the training set, highest mean ROC-AUC.

| Candidate | CV ROC-AUC | CV PR-AUC | |
|---|---|---|---|
| logistic_regression | 0.781 +/- 0.067 | 0.624 | |
| random_forest | 0.793 +/- 0.065 | 0.661 | selected |

* **Decision threshold**: **0.32** (instead of the default 0.50). Chosen by minimising
  expected cost on out-of-fold training predictions, assuming a missed bad loan costs
  5 and a rejected good applicant costs 1
  (the cost matrix published with the dataset; **an assumption, not a measured number**).
  On the training data this lowered the out-of-fold cost from 511
  to 433.

## Data

* German Credit (OpenML credit-g v1 = UCI Statlog German Credit Data): 1,000 loan applications at a German bank, donated by Hans Hofmann in 1994.
  Licence CC BY 4.0 (see `data/README.md`). Bad rate 30%.
* Split: 800 train / 200 test, stratified, `random_state=42`.
* Inputs (17): duration, credit_amount, installment_commitment, residence_since, existing_credits, num_dependents, checking_status, credit_history, purpose, savings_status, employment, other_parties, property_magnitude, other_payment_plans, housing, job, own_telephone.
* **Deliberately excluded**: age, personal_status, foreign_worker (see fairness note).

## Fairness note and protected-attribute ablation

The dataset contains `personal_status` (encodes sex and marital status), `foreign_worker`
(a proxy for national origin) and `age`. Deciding who gets credit on the basis of sex, national
origin or age is discriminatory and illegal in many places (for example the US Equal Credit
Opportunity Act and EU equal-treatment rules), so these columns are **not model inputs**.

What that decision costs, measured with the same 5-fold CV on the training set
(`random_forest`):

| Inputs | CV ROC-AUC |
|---|---|
| without protected attributes (the saved model) | 0.793 +/- 0.065 |
| with age, personal_status, foreign_worker | 0.796 +/- 0.063 |

Difference: +0.003 ROC-AUC. Removing the columns is not a full fairness fix:
other features (job, housing, purpose) can act as proxies. A real audit would compare error rates
across groups; this learning project does not do that.

## Limitations

* 1,000 rows from a German bank in the early 1990s, amounts in Deutsche Mark. It says nothing
  reliable about any current population or lender.
* The test set has only 200 rows (60 bad), so every test
  metric carries sizeable sampling noise; differences of a few hundredths are not meaningful.
* No hyperparameter tuning was done; both candidates use sensible fixed settings.
* The 5:1 cost ratio is an assumption. Change it in `src/scoring.py` and the threshold moves.
* Probabilities from a class-weighted model are not calibrated to the real default rate.

## Intended use

A learning and portfolio project. **Not for real lending decisions.**
